Assam

Discover the ancient monuments, vibrant traditions and natural beauty of Assam.

A Land of Culture and Nature

Assam is a state in northeastern India, known for its tea gardens, the Brahmaputra River, wildlife and rich cultural heritage. Its traditions include the famous Bihu dance, handwoven textiles, traditional crafts and distinctive cuisine. Assam's history reflects the legacy of the Ahom kingdom and the diverse communities that have shaped the region. Preserving these traditions helps future generations understand and appreciate its cultural identity.

Historical Heritage Sites

Assam's temples, historic buildings and archaeological sites reveal its religious, artistic and political history.

Kamakhya Temple

Located on Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Kamakhya Temple is an important Shakti pilgrimage site. It is known for its distinctive temple architecture and the annual Ambubachi Mela.

Rang Ghar

Located in Sivasagar, Rang Ghar is a historic two-storey structure associated with the Ahom rulers. It was used as a royal pavilion for watching traditional games and events.

Talatal Ghar

Talatal Ghar is a historic palace complex in Sivasagar associated with the Ahom kingdom. Its architectural remains provide insight into the region's royal and military history.

Umananda Temple

Umananda Temple stands on Peacock Island in the Brahmaputra River near Guwahati. Dedicated to Lord Shiva, it is a notable religious and cultural landmark of Assam.

Traditions & Folk Culture

Assam's cultural traditions are expressed through folk dance, music, weaving and traditional crafts.

Bihu Dance

Bihu dance is a lively folk dance performed during Rongali Bihu. Its energetic movements and traditional music celebrate the arrival of spring and the agricultural cycle.

Sattriya Dance

Sattriya is a classical dance form that developed in Assam's Vaishnavite monasteries, known as satras. It combines expressive movements, music and devotional storytelling.

Muga Silk Weaving

Assam is famous for Muga silk, known for its natural golden colour. Skilled weavers use this silk to make traditional garments, including the mekhela chador.

Jaapi Craft

The jaapi is a traditional conical hat made using materials such as bamboo and palm leaves. It is a recognised cultural symbol of Assam and is used for decoration and traditional occasions.

Festivals of Assam

Assam's festivals celebrate agriculture, community life, devotion and the changing seasons.

Rongali Bihu

Rongali Bihu, also called Bohag Bihu, marks the Assamese New Year and the arrival of spring. People celebrate with Bihu songs, dances, traditional clothing and festive food.

Bhogali Bihu

Bhogali Bihu, or Magh Bihu, celebrates the harvest. Traditional community feasts, bonfires and foods made from rice are important parts of the festival.

Kongali Bihu

Kongali Bihu, also known as Kati Bihu, is observed during a period when crops are growing in the fields. Lamps are lit in homes and agricultural spaces as a prayer for a good harvest.

Ambubachi Mela

Held at Kamakhya Temple in Guwahati, Ambubachi Mela is a major religious gathering associated with fertility and the natural cycle. It attracts pilgrims from different parts of India.

Traditional Food of Assam

Assamese cuisine is known for rice, fish, fresh herbs, seasonal vegetables and distinctive traditional preparations.

Assamese Thali

A traditional Assamese meal may include rice, dal, leafy vegetables, seasonal dishes, fish and chutneys. The exact combination varies among communities and households.

Masor Tenga

Masor tenga is a popular Assamese sour fish curry. It is prepared with fish and ingredients such as tomatoes or other souring agents, giving it a refreshing flavour.

Til Pitha

Til pitha is a traditional Assamese rice cake filled with sesame seeds and jaggery. It is especially popular during Bhogali Bihu.

Poita Bhat

Poita bhat is a traditional preparation made by soaking cooked rice in water, often overnight. It is commonly eaten with salt, onions, chillies and other accompaniments.

Pitha

Pitha refers to a variety of traditional rice-based snacks and sweets. They may be steamed, roasted or fried and are often prepared during festivals and family gatherings.

Laru

Laru is a traditional sweet ball often prepared from ingredients such as coconut, sesame seeds or puffed rice with jaggery. Different varieties are enjoyed during celebrations.

Preserving Assam's Heritage

Assam's heritage lives in its historic monuments, folk dances, handwoven textiles, festivals, food traditions and community stories.

We can help preserve this cultural wealth by documenting local histories, supporting traditional artists and weavers, caring for historic sites and sharing cultural knowledge with future generations.
