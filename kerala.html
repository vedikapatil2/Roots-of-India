Kerala

Explore the natural beauty, ancient heritage and living traditions of God's Own Country.

A Land of Nature and Tradition

Kerala is a beautiful state in southwestern India, known for its green landscapes, backwaters, temples, historic architecture and rich cultural traditions. Its heritage includes classical dance, traditional martial arts, colourful festivals and distinctive cuisine. Kerala's cultural identity reflects the knowledge, creativity and community life passed down through generations.

Historical Heritage Sites

Kerala's historic palaces, forts and religious monuments reveal the state's architectural beauty and cultural history.

Padmanabhaswamy Temple

Located in Thiruvananthapuram, this famous temple is dedicated to Lord Vishnu. Its traditional Kerala architecture and detailed stonework make it an important religious and cultural landmark.

Bekal Fort

Situated in Kasaragod, Bekal Fort is a large coastal fort overlooking the Arabian Sea. Its defensive structures and sea views reflect Kerala's historic coastal importance.

Mattancherry Palace

Also known as the Dutch Palace, this historic building in Kochi is known for its murals, traditional interiors and connection to the region's royal history.

St. Francis Church

Located in Fort Kochi, St. Francis Church is one of India's historically significant European-built churches. It reflects the maritime connections and colonial history of the Kerala coast.

Traditions & Folk Culture

Kerala preserves its cultural identity through classical dance, traditional performances, martial arts and handicrafts.

Kathakali

Kathakali is a classical dance-drama known for its elaborate costumes, colourful makeup, expressive eyes and hand gestures. It presents stories from Indian epics and traditional literature.

Mohiniyattam

Mohiniyattam is a classical dance form from Kerala, recognised for its graceful movements, gentle expressions and flowing white-and-gold costumes.

Mural Painting

Kerala mural art is known for detailed figures, traditional themes and rich natural colours. These paintings can be found in temples, palaces and cultural collections.

Traditional Handloom

Kerala's handloom traditions include Kasavu fabric, often recognised by its cream or off-white cloth and golden border. These textiles are commonly worn during celebrations.

Festivals of Kerala

Kerala's festivals celebrate harvests, mythology, community life and the state's diverse cultural traditions.

Onam

Onam is Kerala's major harvest festival, associated with the legend of King Mahabali. Celebrations include flower carpets called pookalam, traditional feasts, cultural performances and boat races.

Vishu

Vishu marks the traditional Malayalam New Year celebration for many people in Kerala. The Vishukkani, festive meals and the exchange of Vishukkaineettam are important customs.

Thrissur Pooram

Thrissur Pooram is a famous temple festival known for colourful parasols, traditional percussion ensembles and grand processions.

Vallam Kali

Vallam Kali refers to traditional boat races held in Kerala's waterways. Teams row decorated boats together, accompanied by songs and enthusiastic community support.

Traditional Food of Kerala

Kerala cuisine is known for rice, coconut, spices and traditional recipes passed down through generations.

Kerala Sadya

Sadya is a traditional vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf. It includes rice, curries, vegetables, pickles, papadam and desserts.

Appam with Stew

Appam is a soft rice pancake with delicate edges. It is often served with a mild coconut-milk stew made with vegetables or meat.

Puttu and Kadala Curry

Puttu is made with steamed rice flour and coconut. It is commonly served with kadala curry prepared from black chickpeas and spices.

Malabar Biryani

This popular regional rice dish combines aromatic spices with rice and meat or other ingredients. Recipes vary throughout the Malabar region.

Palada Payasam

Palada payasam is a traditional Kerala dessert made with rice ada, milk and sugar. It is often prepared for festive meals and special occasions.

Banana Chips

Thin slices of banana are fried until crisp to make this popular Kerala snack, which is also served during festivals and special meals.

Preserving Kerala's Heritage

Kerala's heritage lives through its historic monuments, performing arts, handloom traditions, festivals, cuisine and community stories.

We can help preserve this cultural wealth by documenting local traditions, supporting artists and craftspeople, caring for historic sites and sharing traditional knowledge with future generations.
